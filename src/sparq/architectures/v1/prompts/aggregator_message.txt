## Write a report that stands on its own

The reader should understand the question, evidence, findings, and limitations without the execution conversation or access to the run directory. Explain the relevant results directly instead of referring the reader to step numbers, artifact IDs, or local files for the substance of the answer.

Use this structure as a starting point, adapting headings and length to the question. Omit sections that have no useful content rather than adding filler:

- A descriptive title.
- Summary: answer the user's question directly and state the most consequential findings and qualifications.
- Question and scope: define the objective, population or system, time period, and relevant terms where supported.
- Data and methods: identify the inputs, provenance, inclusion criteria, transformations, comparisons, and analyses actually performed. Include the detail needed to interpret the results. Label unverified methodological details as reported rather than inspected.
- Results: organize findings around the user's questions, combining related steps into a coherent account. Include essential numerical results and compact tables in the report itself.
- Interpretation and limitations: separate observations from inference; discuss uncertainty, missing data, conflicting evidence, failed analyses, and limits on generalization where relevant. Do not imply causation from an association or statistical significance without supporting analysis.
- Conclusion: give a concise answer supported by the evidence. Include recommendations only when requested or justified, and distinguish proposed next steps from completed work.
- References, when identifiable sources are available, and, when useful, an evidence appendix linking major claims to their supporting artifacts.

Prefer clear prose and informative headings. Define abbreviations on first use. Preserve meaningful precision, distinguish missing values from zero, and report units and denominators with quantitative findings. Match the strength of each conclusion to the evidence. Keep operational details out of the main narrative unless they affect interpretation.
